Royal Jewelry Museum



The Royal Jewelry Museum is one of the most important museums in Alexandria, located in the Zizinia district. The museum houses a rare collection of jewelry and artifacts belonging to the Muhammad Ali dynasty, which ruled Egypt from the early 19th century until the 1952 revolution.



The building was originally a royal palace built in 1919 for Princess Fatima al-Zahra, one of the princesses of the Alawite dynasty. It was later converted into a museum after the nationalization of the royal family's properties following the 1952 revolution, and was officially opened as a museum in 1986.

The palace consists of two wings connected by a corridor, and features a luxurious European architectural style, particularly the Renaissance style. It also contains distinctive artistic decorations and stained glass windows, making it an architectural masterpiece as well as a museum.



The museum houses a large number of rare artifacts, most notably: diamond-encrusted crowns, necklaces, luxury watches, medals, decorations, and personal items and utensils made of gold and silver belonging to the kings and princes of the dynasty. Among the most prominent exhibits are the belongings of King Farouk I, such as his gold cigarette case, pocket watch, and royal crown, as well as jewelry belonging to Queen Farida and Princess Fawzia. These items are displayed in glass cases distributed across several halls, each hall containing a collection of jewelry or personal belongings of a member of the royal family, while preserving the original palace layout and interior decorations.

The museum's importance lies in the fact that it documents an important aspect of modern Egyptian history, showing the level of wealth and luxury in which the royal family lived, and reflecting the artistic taste in jewelry design during that period.





The Royal Jewelry Museum is one of the most prominent tourist attractions in Alexandria, combining historical, artistic, and architectural value, and giving visitors a clear picture of the life of the ruling class in Egypt before the establishment of the republican system.
