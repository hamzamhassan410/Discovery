Catacombs of Kom El Shoqafa



It is one of the most important archaeological sites in the city of Alexandria, located in the Karmouz district. It consists of underground tombs dating back to the Roman era, specifically to the second century AD, and was used for several centuries as a burial place.



The Kom El Shoqafa catacombs were named so because of the large quantities of pottery shards found at the site. In ancient times, visitors would bring food and drink when visiting the dead, then break the vessels after use and leave them there. These shards accumulated and formed a mound-like shape.

The tombs were discovered by chance in 1901 AD when a donkey fell into a hole in the ground, revealing the entrance. Since then, they have become one of the most prominent tourist and archaeological sites in Egypt, and were also classified as one of the wonders of the medieval world.



The tombs consist of three levels carved into the rock, reaching a depth of approximately 30 meters below ground level. Part of the lower level is submerged. A circular entrance leads into the tombs, followed by a spiral staircase that was used to lower the bodies. The tombs feature a complex architectural design comprising several main sections, including a circular hall known as the rotunda, a dining hall used for funerary feasts, the main burial chamber containing sarcophagi and sculptures, and another hall containing human and animal skeletal remains.

The most distinctive feature of the Kom El Shoqafa catacombs is their unique artistic style, which blends ancient Egyptian, Greek, and Roman civilizations. The inscriptions and statues showcase a fusion of Pharaonic symbols with Roman and Greek artistic styles, reflecting Alexandria's diverse cultural center during that era.





Initially, the cemeteries were designated for a single family from the wealthy class, but they later became mass graves for a large number of people and were also used for religious rites and memorial ceremonies for the dead.



These tombs are of great importance because they represent a late stage in the development of burial arts in Egypt, demonstrate the cultural interaction between different civilizations, and provide a clear idea of ​​the customs and traditions of society in Roman-era Alexandria.
