Prophet Daniel Street



Prophet Daniel Street is one of the most famous and oldest streets in Alexandria. Located in the heart of the city, it is considered a vibrant street that combines history, culture, and commerce.


The street was named after the Prophet Daniel, as there is a shrine attributed to him within the street. Despite differing opinions about the validity of the existence of his remains in this place, the name has remained associated with him throughout the ages.



The street dates back to ancient times. It is believed to have been part of the Greek city plan during the reign of Alexander the Great, and it has witnessed significant development during the Roman and Islamic eras, and even into modern times, making it a witness to a long history of cultural changes.

Nabi Daniel Street is distinguished by its religious and cultural diversity. It houses a number of different places of worship that reflect the spirit of tolerance and coexistence in Alexandria, most notably the Nabi Daniel Mosque and the Eliyahu Hanavi Synagogue. This demonstrates the historical convergence of multiple religions and cultures in one place. The street is also known as an important cultural center, with numerous bookstores selling new and used books, making it a destination for students and knowledge seekers. Furthermore, it is a bustling commercial street with many shops serving the city's residents and visitors.



Therefore, Nabi Daniel Street is not just an ordinary street, but a symbol of Alexandria's history and culture, combining the charm of the past with the vibrancy of the present, and reflecting a clear image of the diversity of Alexandrian society and its rich history.
